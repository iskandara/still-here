How to cut down on smoking when cold turkey isn't for you

By Aradnaksi, who smokes and is cutting down · Last updated 2 November 2026

I smoke about a pack a day. I know I can't quit cold turkey, so I'm trying something smaller: to cut down on smoking until a pack lasts me three days instead of one. This page is what I've learned about doing that, from the research and from building Still Here, the app I use to track it. The full story of why I started is here.

I'm not a doctor. Everything health-related below comes from the sources listed at the end, and a quitline, doctor or pharmacist can help you more than any page can.

Does cutting down actually help you quit?

Yes, as a way to get there. A 2019 Cochrane review looked at 22 studies, with 9,219 people, that compared the two approaches, and found that cutting down before quitting led to about the same long-term quit rates as stopping all at once. People who cut down with help from fast-acting nicotine replacement or a prescribed stop-smoking medicine may do better than people who cut down without it.

That matters if you're like me. Health advice often treats stopping on a set day as the only real option, and if that feels impossible, it's easy to do nothing. The evidence says a gradual route is a fair way in.

The honest part: fewer isn't the same as safe

Cutting down is a route to stopping, not a safe place to stay. A large 2018 review in the BMJ, covering 141 studies, found that smoking just one cigarette a day still carries a big share of the extra risk of heart disease and stroke that comes with smoking 20 a day. For men, one a day came with about 46% of the extra heart disease risk. For women, it was about 31%. The authors' conclusion was that there's no safe level of smoking.

I don't find that discouraging. I find it useful: it tells me to treat each smaller number as a step, and to keep going past it.

How to cut down on smoking, step by step

  1. Count a normal week first

    Before changing anything, log every cigarette for a week. Most of us guess low. A real number is the only fair starting point, and it makes every later drop visible.

  2. Set a limit you believe you can keep

    Pick a target that feels a little hard, not heroic. Mine is to go from about twenty a day towards a pack every three days, in stages. You can think in days, weeks or months; a weekly or monthly limit leaves room for one rough day without wrecking the plan.

  3. Find out when you smoke

    Look at your log for patterns: the time of day, the day of the week, what you were doing. Those are your tricky moments. Still Here records the time of every tap and shows them as charts, so the pattern shows up without any extra effort.

  4. Put a pause before the one that tips you over

    When you're about to go over your limit, wait ten minutes before deciding. Sometimes the urge passes; sometimes it doesn't, and that's fine too. When a tap would take you past today's limit, Still Here offers that ten-minute wait first.

  5. Drop the easiest ones first

    Not every cigarette matters to you equally. The ones you smoke out of habit, or just because you're bored, are usually easier to let go than the one with your morning coffee. Start there, and leave the hard ones for later.

  6. Get some help

    A quitline counsellor can help you plan a cut-down, not just a quit day. A pharmacist or doctor can tell you whether nicotine replacement or a stop-smoking medicine suits you; the Cochrane review found these may help people who are cutting down.

  7. Pick a quit date when you're ready

    The studies in the Cochrane review were about cutting down to quit. Once your number is low and steady, choosing a day to stop completely is the step that brings the biggest health gain.

When you slip

You will have days over your limit. I do. Most trackers count a perfect streak and reset you to zero the moment you slip, and for something you're reducing, that means failing constantly. A bad day is one day. The trend over weeks is what tells you whether it's working.

A slip is one day. It doesn't undo the ones before it.
